# Platform policies relevant to manual account warm-up: Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn

Research date: 2026-08-31. All findings below come from first-party policy pages (Meta Transparency Center, help.instagram.com, tiktok.com legal and community-guidelines pages, help.x.com, linkedin.com/legal and LinkedIn Help). Where a page blocked direct fetching (help.x.com returns 403 to non-browser clients; help.instagram.com and the relocated TikTok community-guidelines pages render client-side), the text was retrieved from Wayback Machine snapshots of the first-party URL; each such case is marked "via archive snapshot" with the snapshot date. The content itself is first-party in every citation. Policy-page dates are noted where the page publishes one. No third-party numbers are reported as policy facts.

## Executive summary

- Manual, hand-performed niche engagement (liking, following, reposting, commenting) by a brand owner on their own brand account is not prohibited by any of the four platforms' published policies, provided the account identity is not deceptive, the engagement is genuine (not exchanged, purchased, or coordinated), and the pace is not extreme. The risky perimeter is well documented; the safe interior is mostly policy silence.
- All four platforms prohibit roughly the same warm-up failure modes: buying/selling/exchanging engagement, reciprocal engagement agreements ("like-for-like", pods), aggressive or indiscriminate following and follow/unfollow churn, high-frequency action bursts (Meta prohibits acting "at very high frequencies" even manually), duplicate accounts amplifying the same content, misleading identity, and ban evasion via new accounts.
- Account-model rules differ and matter for MarketingOS: X explicitly allows up to 10 accounts including business accounts and third-party "social media manager" operation; TikTok allows multiple accounts if not used deceptively; Instagram's terms do not restrict account count but require accurate information and prohibit automated account creation and account sales; LinkedIn allows exactly one member profile, in the member's real name, so a brand presence must not be a fake personal profile.
- Disclosure at posting time is now a first-party platform rule on three of the four platforms, not just a legal (FTC/ASA) matter: Instagram requires the branded content tool per its Terms of Use; TikTok requires its commercial content disclosure toggle even when promoting your own business; X has a dedicated Paid Partnerships Policy requiring its "Paid Partnership" content-disclosure setting. LinkedIn requires a "clear and conspicuous notice" for compensated endorsements in its Professional Community Policies.
- Only X publishes concrete first-party action-volume numbers (400 follows/day technical limit, 5,000-follow ratio threshold, post and DM daily limits). Meta, TikTok, and LinkedIn publish that limits exist but not the numbers. Any specific daily caps MarketingOS ships for Instagram, TikTok, or LinkedIn are judgment calls, not platform facts, and must be labeled as such.

## Instagram (Meta)

Meta's Community Standards apply to Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and Threads as one document set; the old Instagram Community Guidelines URL now resolves to the Meta Community Standards index (observed 2026-08-31). [Community Standards](https://help.instagram.com/477434105621119)

### Account creation and identity

- The Instagram Terms of Use (page shows "Effective Date: 1 January 2025"; retrieved via archive snapshot of 2026-05-31 because help.instagram.com renders client-side) require accurate and up-to-date registration information, prohibit impersonating "someone or something you aren't", and prohibit creating an account for someone else "unless you have their express permission". A solo operator creating an account for a brand they own satisfies this. [Instagram Terms of Use](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870)
- The same terms prohibit creating accounts "in an automated way without our express permission" and prohibit buying, selling, or transferring any aspect of an account, including the username. Manual creation by the owner is the compliant path; purchased or pre-warmed accounts from third parties are not. [Instagram Terms of Use](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870)
- Meta's Authentic Identity Representation standard (change log shows an update dated Aug 29, 2026) prohibits impersonation and "identity misrepresentation to mislead or deceive others" everywhere, but its stricter rules - one account per person, no accounts representing "a non-human entity, such as a business", no multiple accounts - are explicitly scoped "On Facebook". The policy text does not apply those Facebook-specific restrictions to Instagram, where business accounts are a supported product. [Authentic Identity Representation](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/authentic-identity-representation/)
- Instagram professional accounts are the surface Meta itself names for creators/businesses that want to post branded content or run partnership ads. [Eligibility requirements for partnership ads and branded content](https://help.instagram.com/116947042301556) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-24)
- Account Integrity (change log shows May 28, 2026) allows Meta to restrict accounts "owned by the same person or entity as an account that has been disabled" and accounts "created or repurposed to evade a previous account or entity removal". A banned brand account must not simply be recreated. It also says Meta may demand more information when an account is created "through automated means, such as scripting". [Account Integrity](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/account-integrity/)

### Spam and inauthentic behavior (what warm-up must avoid)

- The Spam standard (change log's most recent dated entry: Jun 27, 2024) prohibits "Posting, sharing, engaging with content or creating accounts... either manually or automatically, at very high frequencies". This is the single most important sentence for warm-up design: manual does not equal safe if the frequency is abnormal, and Meta "may place restrictions on accounts that are acting at lower frequencies when other indicators of Spam (e.g., posting repetitive content) or signals of inauthenticity are present". [Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)
- The same policy prohibits buying/selling/exchanging accounts and prohibits exchanging anything of value for engagement ("likes, shares, views, follows, clicks, use of specific hashtags"), including engagement giveaways ("Anyone that likes my page will be entered to win $500") and like/share-gating. Warm-up must not solicit engagement in exchange for anything. [Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)
- The Inauthentic Behavior standard (change log shows Dec 12, 2025) prohibits networks of inauthentic assets controlled by the same individual used to "deceive Meta or our users about the identity, or origin of an audience or the entity that they represent" and Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior. A single transparent brand account run by its owner is outside this; fleets of pseudo-independent accounts amplifying one brand are inside it. [Inauthentic Behavior](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior/)

### Disclosure when posting commercial content

- The Terms of Use state: "If you post branded content, you must comply with our Branded Content Policies, which require you to use our branded content tool." So on Instagram the paid-partnership label is a terms-level obligation, not a suggestion. [Instagram Terms of Use](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870)
- To post organic branded content or run partnership ads, accounts "must pass and remain compliant with our eligibility requirements", including using an Instagram professional account, complying with Community Standards, and having "an authentic, established presence... includes maintaining a sufficient follower base" (no number published). Note the direct product implication: a warmed but brand-new account may not yet be eligible for branded content features. [Eligibility requirements](https://help.instagram.com/116947042301556) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-24)
- Note on scope: the branded content tool governs promotion of third-party brands in exchange for value. A brand account posting about its own products is ordinary commercial content on Instagram; no first-party page reviewed requires a label for self-promotion (contrast TikTok below).

### First-party rate limits

- None published with numbers on the pages reviewed. Meta's spam policy names "very high frequencies" without quantifying, and Instagram help pages on follow limits could not be retrieved in readable form (client-side rendering, archive snapshots empty). Treat all Instagram action-volume numbers circulating online as folklore. [Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)

## TikTok

The current Community Guidelines show "Released August 14, 2025 / Effective September 13, 2025". The guidelines have moved to tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/, which renders client-side; text was retrieved from an archive snapshot (2026-03-06) of the first-party community-guidelines URL. [Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)

### Account creation and identity

- Multiple accounts are explicitly allowed: "You can have multiple accounts - for example, for fan content or creative expression - but not to deceive others or break the rules." A transparent brand account owned and run by the operator fits this. [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- Prohibited: "Pretending to be a fake person or organization with the goal of misleading people" and impersonation without a fan/parody disclosure in the display name. The brand account must actually represent the brand it claims. [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- Ban circumvention is prohibited: no new or alternative account to continue after a ban or to bypass restrictions; TikTok "may ban any alternative accounts being used or new accounts that are created". [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)

### Spam and fake engagement (what warm-up must avoid)

- "We strictly prohibit automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems." All warm-up actions must be performed by hand, which matches the MarketingOS model. [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- Prohibited spam behaviors include "Using automation to run many accounts or send repetitive content", "Posting a large amount of irrelevant material", and "Buying or selling followers or engagement for financial gain". Also prohibited: "Trading, marketing, or providing access to services that artificially increase engagement" and "Sharing how-to guides or tips for boosting engagement in fake or deceptive ways" - relevant to how MarketingOS words its own warm-up documentation and any public marketing of the feature. [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- "Like-for-like" promises and false incentives for engagement make content ineligible for the For You feed; inauthentically inflated metrics are removed. Reciprocal engagement schemes are therefore out even at small scale. [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- "Engagement services like selling likes or followers" are also listed as a prohibited trade under Regulated Goods and Services. [Regulated Goods, Services, and Commercial Activities](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- Coordinated networks of accounts working together "to mislead people or our systems" (covert influence operations) are prohibited; this is the ceiling that a multi-account-per-brand strategy would eventually hit. [Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)

### Disclosure when posting commercial content

- TikTok is the strictest of the four: "If you're posting commercial content on TikTok, you must clearly disclose it using the content disclosure setting", and disclosure is required both when "Promoting your own business, product, or service" and when posting incentivized branded content. Undisclosed commercial content is For You feed ineligible; repeated failure "can lead to your account being temporarily restricted from posting content, or can lead to an account ban". This means even ordinary self-promotional brand posts after warm-up need the toggle. [Commercial Disclosure and Paid Marketing](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)
- The Branded Content Policy (fetched live 2026-08-31; page shows "Published: 4 August 2026, Effective date: 31 August 2026" - i.e. effective as of the research date) defines branded content as promotion of a third-party brand for payment or any incentive (gifts, commissions, affiliate links, ambassador relationships), requires the commercial content disclosure toggle, requires the promoted product to be identifiable without leaving the post, and lists prohibited industries (adult, tobacco/nicotine, drugs, weapons, weight loss, political advertising, professional services, and others). [Branded Content Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en)
- Paid political advertising is banned outright, including compensated creator political content. [Civic and Election Integrity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity) (via archive snapshot 2026-03-06)

### First-party rate limits

- None published. TikTok's guidelines describe qualitative thresholds ("large amount", "high-volume", "repetitive") without numbers. Any TikTok daily caps in MarketingOS are judgment calls.

## X (Twitter)

help.x.com returned HTTP 403 to this environment's fetcher, so all X pages were retrieved from Wayback Machine snapshots of the first-party URLs (snapshot dates noted per page). Note also that help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/platform-manipulation now redirects (HTTP 301, observed in a 2026-08-28 crawl) to the consolidated Authenticity policy. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)

### Account creation, identity, and multiple accounts

- The Authenticity policy (page header shows "April 2025"; via archive snapshot 2026-08-26) requires accounts to be "legitimate, genuine and transparent as to their source, identity, and popularity". Real-name display is not required, but "fake personas" - manufactured identities with stock/stolen/AI-generated photos or misleading profile information used to deceive - are prohibited. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)
- Multiple accounts are explicitly allowed and quantified: "X allows users to create and/or operate up to ten (10) accounts for different, non-duplicative purposes", with allowed examples including "accounts for personal projects, hobbies/interests, or for your business(es)" and controlling "multiple accounts on behalf of a third-party (e.g. 'Social Media Managers')". An operator running their own personal account plus a brand account is expressly within policy. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)
- Prohibited: operating multiple accounts that "interact with the same or substantially similar content" or that cross-post substantially identical content, to inflate prominence. One brand, one account per purpose; do not have the personal and brand accounts systematically boost each other's posts. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)
- Ban evasion is prohibited, including "creating new accounts", "repurposing an already-existing account", and "having someone else operate an account on your behalf" to evade enforcement. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)

### Spam and engagement manipulation (what warm-up must avoid)

- Content spam prohibitions include bulk/aggressive unsolicited replies and mentions, "promoting content by replying with content that is irrelevant to the topic of the original post", excessive unrelated hashtags, repetitive identical posts ("Copypasta"), and posting-and-deleting the same content repeatedly. Warm-up comments must be on-topic replies, not promotional boilerplate. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)
- Engagement spam prohibitions include "coordinating to exchange engagement in any X features, such as Likes, Polls, Replies, Reposts, Lists, Views, or Follows", compensating others to inflate metrics, "follow churn" (following then unfollowing large numbers to inflate follower count), "indiscriminate following - following and/or unfollowing a large number of unrelated accounts in a short time period", "engaging with posts aggressively... to drive traffic or attention to accounts, websites, products, services, or initiatives", and buying/selling/trading accounts. [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)
- Enforcement explicitly includes anti-spam challenges "When we detect suspicious levels of activity", reach restriction, feature limits, and suspension; for multiple-account violations "you may be asked to choose one account to keep". [Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)

### Disclosure when posting commercial content

- X now has a first-party Paid Partnerships Policy (no date on page; via archive snapshot 2026-07-31; third-party reporting places the feature rollout in March 2026, noted here only for timing context, not as policy): posts that are part of a paid partnership "published as an organic Post must have the 'Paid Partnership' disclosure on", set via the Content Disclosure settings. Paid partnership is defined broadly: gifted products, monetary or in-kind compensation, affiliate commissions, and brand-ambassador relationships. Users remain responsible for FTC and other legal compliance. [Paid Partnerships Policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/paid-partnerships-policy)
- The policy lists industries ineligible for paid partnership promotion (adult, alcohol, drugs, supplements, pharma, tobacco, weapons, weight loss, political/social-issue promotion for commercial purposes), with additional UK/EU/Australia restrictions (notably financial products and gambling), and the UK section requires disclosure even for "own-brand posts created by a user who has a commercial interest in the relevant brand (for example, as a director)". Enforcement ranges from required post removal to suspension of accounts whose sole purpose is violating the policy. [Paid Partnerships Policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/paid-partnerships-policy)

### First-party rate limits (X is the only platform that publishes numbers)

- From "About X limits" (via archive snapshot 2026-06-08): 500 DMs sent/day; 50 original posts and 200 replies/day for unverified accounts (broken into semi-hourly sub-limits); 4 account-email changes/hour; technical follow limit of 400/day; follow-ratio limits after following 5,000 accounts. The page still references a legacy "post limit of 2,400 updates per day" in its troubleshooting text. These are described as technical limits, "and there are additional rules prohibiting aggressive following behavior" below them. [About X limits](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-limits)
- From "About following on X" (page dated March 2019; via archive snapshot 2026-05-17): no cap on total follows overall, 400 follows/day, 5,000-account ratio threshold, and an explicit list of prohibited follow behaviors (churn, indiscriminate following, duplicating another account's followers, third-party follower services). Hitting limits or behaving aggressively can lock the account. [About following on X](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit)
- Important reading: 400/day is a technical ceiling, not a safe harbor; the same pages say aggressive following below the ceiling is still a rules violation. MarketingOS caps should sit far below technical limits.

## LinkedIn

### Account creation and identity

- The User Agreement (page shows "Effective on November 3, 2025"; fetched live 2026-08-31) requires: "you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name", and the Don'ts prohibit creating "a false identity", creating "a Member profile for anyone other than yourself (a real person)", and sharing an account. There is no compliant way to create a LinkedIn member profile "for the brand"; the brand presence must be an organization Page attached to the operator's real profile, and warm-up engagement on LinkedIn is done by a real named person. [User Agreement, sections 2.1-2.2 and 8](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- The Professional Community Policies (no date shown; fetched live 2026-08-31) add: no fake profiles or entities, no misleading information about "yourself, your business, your qualifications... affiliations", and "Do not associate yourself on LinkedIn with a business or organization that you are not actually professionally associated with". The owner-operator relationship should be truthful on the profile. [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)

### Spam and inauthentic engagement (what warm-up must avoid)

- The PCP spam section is unusually specific about reciprocal engagement: "Don't do things to artificially increase engagement with your content. Respond authentically to others' content and don't agree with others ahead of time to like or re-share each other's content." Engagement pods and like-exchange arrangements are named policy violations. [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)
- Also prohibited: untargeted, irrelevant, gratuitously repetitive messages or content, and using the invitation feature "to send promotional messages to people you don't know or to otherwise spam people". Warm-up connection requests must be relevant, not promotional blasts. [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)
- The User Agreement Don'ts prohibit "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement", and prohibit scraping. Every warm-up action must be performed by the human in the normal UI; even scheduling tools may not perform the platform action. [User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)

### Disclosure when posting commercial content

- PCP: "Do not share content or endorse someone or something in exchange for personal benefit (including personal or family relationships, monetary payment, free products or services, or other value), unless you have included a clear and conspicuous notice of the personal benefit you receive and have otherwise complied with our Advertising Policies." LinkedIn has no organic paid-partnership toggle equivalent on the pages reviewed; the disclosure is textual. Political endorsements in exchange for value are banned outright. [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies) and [Advertising Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/ads-policy)

### First-party rate limits

- LinkedIn confirms invitation limits exist but publishes almost no numbers: "LinkedIn limits the number of invitations you can send"; restrictions trigger when "You've sent many invitations within a short amount of time" or many invitations are ignored or marked spam, and suspected automation leads to suspension. The one published number: "the network size limit for LinkedIn members is a maximum of 30,000 1st-degree connections". Withdrawn invitations cannot be resent to the same person for up to three weeks. (Help page shows "Last updated: 9 months ago" as of 2026-08-31.) [Types of restrictions for sending invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012)

## Cross-platform answers to the four questions

1. **Is manual brand-account creation and hand-performed niche engagement permitted?** Yes on all four platforms, with conditions. Instagram: accurate info, no impersonation, no automated creation, no purchased accounts ([ToU](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870)). TikTok: multiple accounts fine if non-deceptive ([CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)). X: up to 10 non-duplicative accounts, business accounts and managed accounts expressly allowed ([Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)). LinkedIn: one real-name member account; brand presence must not be a personal profile ([User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). No platform prohibits genuine manual likes, follows, reposts, or on-topic comments as such.
2. **What must a warm-up checklist avoid?** The shared prohibited set: buying/selling/exchanging engagement or accounts (all four); reciprocal engagement agreements - Meta "exchanging for engagement" ([Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)), X "coordinating to exchange engagement" ([Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)), TikTok "like-for-like" ([CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)), LinkedIn "don't agree with others ahead of time" ([PCP](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)); follow churn and indiscriminate following (X, explicitly; Meta via "very high frequencies"); very-high-frequency action bursts even when manual (Meta, explicitly); irrelevant or repetitive comments used promotionally (X content spam; LinkedIn spam); multiple own accounts engaging with the same content (X; Meta inauthentic behavior; TikTok CIO); engagement solicitation with incentives (Meta spam; TikTok FYF rules); ban evasion by re-creating accounts (all four).
3. **Disclosure when the account later posts brand/commercial content:** Instagram - branded content tool mandatory via ToU, professional account plus eligibility required ([ToU](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870), [eligibility](https://help.instagram.com/116947042301556)). TikTok - content disclosure toggle mandatory for all commercial content including self-promotion, plus Branded Content Policy industry bans ([CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities), [BC Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en)). X - "Paid Partnership" content disclosure mandatory for incentivized organic posts ([Paid Partnerships Policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/paid-partnerships-policy)). LinkedIn - clear and conspicuous textual notice for compensated endorsements ([PCP](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies)).
4. **First-party rate limits:** X publishes numbers (400 follows/day, 5,000 ratio threshold, 500 DMs/day, 50 posts + 200 replies/day unverified) ([X limits](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-limits), [following](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit)). LinkedIn publishes that invitation limits exist and a 30,000-connection cap, without invitation numbers ([invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012)). Meta and TikTok publish qualitative thresholds only. No other numeric caps were found on first-party pages.

## Unresolved (policy is silent or could not be verified first-party)

- **Instagram numeric action limits.** No first-party page reviewed publishes daily like/follow/comment numbers. Instagram help articles on follow limits and on adding multiple accounts could not be retrieved in readable form (client-side rendering defeated both direct fetch and archive snapshots), so the commonly cited figures (e.g. a 7,500 following cap, 5 logged-in accounts) remain unverified here and must not be stated as policy facts.
- **"Warming up" as an activity.** No platform policy mentions account warm-up, positively or negatively. Genuine niche observation (scrolling, watching, searching) has no visible policy surface at all. The activity is judged by its observable behaviors, which is why the prohibited-behavior list above is the real spec.
- **What counts as "very high frequencies" (Meta), "aggressive" (X), or "many invitations within a short amount of time" (LinkedIn).** Deliberately unpublished, enforcement-defined thresholds. Any cap MarketingOS picks is a conservative guess.
- **Instagram self-promotion disclosure.** Meta requires the branded content tool for third-party paid partnerships; no reviewed first-party page addresses labeling of purely self-promotional posts by the brand's own account (TikTok, by contrast, requires disclosure for self-promotion).
- **Whether X's new Paid Partnership toggle is available to all accounts.** The policy text (archived 2026-07-31) mandates the disclosure but does not state eligibility conditions; third-party reporting (not first-party, not relied on) suggests a staged rollout with eligibility requirements. Verify in-product before making it a hard checklist gate.
- **LinkedIn Pages mechanics.** The conclusion that a brand should be a Page rather than a profile follows from the one-real-person-profile rule; the specific Pages product rules (admin requirements, Page eligibility) were not separately reviewed.
- **Meta Branded Content Policies full text.** The ToU links to facebook.com/policies/brandedcontent, which blocked both direct fetch and archive fetch (login/browser wall). The obligation to use the branded content tool is verified from the ToU itself; the policy's internal detail is not independently verified here.

## Recommendations for the readiness checklist and default caps

Everything numeric below that is not cited to a platform page is a conservative judgment call, not a policy fact, and should be labeled that way in the product.

**Rule in (supported by policy or policy-silent and low risk):**
- Manual account creation by the brand owner with accurate registration info, real ownership, and truthful profile/bio; on LinkedIn, a Page plus the operator's real profile, never a persona profile.
- Observation sessions (scrolling, watching, searching niche content) without engagement - no policy surface found on any platform.
- Genuine, on-topic manual likes, follows of relevant accounts, reposts, and substantive comments that respond to the post's actual content.
- One account per platform per brand as the default. X permits up to 10 non-duplicative accounts and explicitly permits manager-operated accounts, but duplicates that engage with the same content are prohibited everywhere.

**Rule out (explicitly prohibited somewhere, so ban in the checklist):**
- Any automation that performs a platform action (like, follow, comment, post, DM, invite) - prohibited by LinkedIn's User Agreement and TikTok's guidelines outright, and a spam signal on Meta and X. Scheduling and recording human work is fine; executing it is not.
- Buying accounts, followers, likes, or any engagement; using or promoting engagement services.
- Reciprocal engagement arrangements of any size, including informal "I'll like yours if you like mine" deals and pods.
- Follow/unfollow churn and indiscriminate mass following; unfollowing campaigns to reshape ratios.
- Engagement solicitation with incentives (giveaways conditioned on likes/follows, like-gating).
- Comment templates or copy-paste comments; promotional replies unrelated to the post topic.
- Recreating an account after a ban or restriction, on the same or a different identity.
- Cross-amplification between the operator's personal account and the brand account as a systematic tactic (X names this pattern specifically).

**Constrain (judgment-call defaults, to be labeled as such):**
- Per-account daily caps well below any known technical ceiling, with randomized allowed windows and mandatory idle days. For X the only published anchors are technical (400 follows/day; 50 posts + 200 replies/day unverified) and X says aggressive behavior below them still violates rules; a warm-up cap should be a small fraction of these. For Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn there are no published numbers at all, so caps are pure risk posture - keep them low, make them configurable, and never present them as platform-sanctioned.
- Ramp gradually: an account's first days should be observation-heavy and engagement-light, because Meta explicitly restricts accounts acting at high frequency "when other indicators... or signals of inauthenticity are present", and new accounts have the weakest authenticity signals.
- LinkedIn invitations: small daily counts, personalized, only to relevant people; LinkedIn restricts on volume-in-short-time and on ignored/spam-marked invitations, and unpublished weekly limits exist.
- Gate the transition to commercial posting on a disclosure checklist per platform: Instagram professional account + branded content tool for any third-party partnership; TikTok disclosure toggle on every commercial post including self-promotion; X Paid Partnership disclosure for any incentivized post; LinkedIn clear textual disclosure for any compensated endorsement. Record the disclosure state as part of the post's audit trail.
- Record per-action evidence (timestamp, action type, target) so that if a platform challenges the account, the operator can demonstrate human, good-faith activity; this also enforces the caps.
- Never market the warm-up feature as increasing reach, evading detection, or making accounts "safe" - TikTok explicitly prohibits sharing tips for boosting engagement "in fake or deceptive ways", and platform enforcement outcomes cannot be guaranteed by any vendor.
