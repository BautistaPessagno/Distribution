# Research platform policies for warm-up actions

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

For the platforms the dogfood Account Slots will target (Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn as candidates), what do current first-party platform policies say about the specific manual actions on the Account Operations readiness checklist (account creation for a brand, niche observation sessions, likes, follows, reposts, comments), and which actions or volumes should the default daily caps and the disclosure checklist therefore rule in, rule out, or constrain? First-party policy pages only, with dates; policies change, so every observation must be dated per the honesty invariants.

## Answer

Full findings with dated first-party citations: [platform-policies-warmup.md](../research/platform-policies-warmup.md). The gist for the Account Operations defaults:

- **Ruled in.** Manual brand-account creation by the owner and genuine hand engagement (likes, follows, reposts, comments) are permitted on all four platforms when identity is accurate and engagement is genuine. X explicitly allows up to 10 non-duplicative accounts including manager-operated business accounts; TikTok allows multiple non-deceptive accounts; Instagram requires accurate info and bans automated creation and account purchases; LinkedIn allows exactly one real-name member profile, so a brand presence there must be a Page, never a persona profile.
- **Ruled out.** Buying, selling, or exchanging engagement; reciprocal engagement agreements and pods; follow/unfollow churn and indiscriminate following; incentivized engagement solicitation; duplicate accounts amplifying the same content; recreating banned accounts. Meta's spam standard prohibits acting "at very high frequencies" even manually, so hand-performed does not mean unconstrained.
- **Disclosure.** Platform-level rules, not just regulator rules: Instagram's terms mandate the branded content tool; TikTok mandates its disclosure toggle even for self-promotion (new Branded Content Policy effective 2026-08-31); X requires its Paid Partnership setting; LinkedIn requires clear textual disclosure. The disclosure checklist item from the Account Operations decision maps to these per platform.
- **Caps.** Only X publishes numbers (400 follows/day, 5,000-follow ratio threshold, daily post and DM limits); LinkedIn publishes a 30,000-connection cap. Instagram and TikTok publish only qualitative thresholds, so MarketingOS default caps for those platforms are labeled judgment calls in the product, never presented as platform facts, per the honesty invariants.
- **Unresolved** (documented in the file): Instagram numeric action limits, Meta Branded Content Policies full text (login-walled), X Paid Partnership toggle eligibility. Some pages were content-verified through dated archive snapshots of first-party URLs; each is labeled inline.
